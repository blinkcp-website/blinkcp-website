**Slide breakdown:**

**Caption:**
Most investors treat DSCR like a pass/fail test. It's not — it's a dial, and where you land on it changes what you can actually borrow.

Below 1.0, rent doesn't fully cover the debt payment on its own — still financeable, but it changes the conversation. Land in the 1.00–1.24 range and you're in the common qualifying band most DSCR programs look for. Push past 1.25 and you're in strong territory, which is where leverage tends to get most favorable: up to 80% LTV on a purchase or rate-and-term refinance, 75% LTV if you're pulling cash out.

## Post 3 — Friday — Market / Thought-Leadership Hot Take

**Format:** Single static image (navy/gold branded graphic, no photo)

**Hook:** "Your construction loan was never supposed to be a 30-year loan. That's the next one."

**Caption:**
Hot take: the investors who get stuck after a build are usually the ones who forgot their construction loan was a bridge, not a destination.

Ground-up construction financing is built to be short — 12 to 24 months, just long enough to get a property from a lot to a finished, rentable asset. It's not supposed to be where your capital sits for the next three decades. Once the property is built and leased (or lease-ready), that's the moment to refinance into a DSCR rental loan — 30-year fixed, qualified on the property's own rent instead of your personal income — so the same lender that financed the build can carry the long-term hold.

Build short. Hold long. Two different loans, two different jobs, no reason to shop them separately.

Wrapping up a build and thinking about the long-term hold? blinkcp.com or DM "REFI."
