DSCR Loan Reserve Requirements: How Much Cash You Need in the Bank Before Closing

October 25, 2026 5 min read

Your down payment gets a property to the closing table. Reserves are a separate, often overlooked requirement that determines whether you're actually ready to close -- liquid cash a lender wants to see sitting in your accounts, untouched, as proof you can cover the mortgage if rent income hiccups. Investors who've budgeted precisely for a down payment and closing costs sometimes get surprised at the eleventh hour by a reserve requirement they didn't plan for. Here's how it actually works.

What Counts as "Reserves"

Reserves are liquid or near-liquid assets a lender verifies you hold, separate from the funds you're using to close the loan. Checking and savings balances almost always count. Many DSCR lenders will also count a portion of stocks, bonds, and retirement account balances, sometimes at a discounted value to account for volatility or early-withdrawal penalties. What generally doesn't count: unsecured lines of credit, funds borrowed specifically for the transaction, or illiquid assets like equity in another property you can't quickly access.

How the Requirement Is Sized

Reserves are typically expressed in months of PITIA -- principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and association dues -- on the subject property, not a flat dollar figure.

A lender requiring six months of reserves on a property with a $2,400 monthly PITIA payment wants to see roughly $14,400 in verified liquid assets, on top of your down payment and closing costs. The exact number of months varies by lender and by the specifics of the deal: your DSCR ratio, the loan-to-value, whether the property is a short-term rental, and how many financed properties you already hold can all push the requirement up or down.

Why Lenders Require It At All

A DSCR loan is underwritten on the property's rental income, which means the lender's main risk is a gap between tenants, a slow lease-up on a new purchase, or an unexpected repair that interrupts cash flow. Reserves exist to cover exactly that gap -- a cushion that lets you keep making payments without missing one while you re-lease a vacant unit or handle a repair, rather than a test of your overall net worth.

Per-Property vs. Portfolio-Wide Reserves

Single propertyReserves are typically calculated only against that property's own PITIA
Growing portfolioMany lenders layer in additional reserve requirements tied to your other financed properties, not just the one you're closing on
Cash-out refinanceReserve requirements often apply on top of, not instead of, seasoning and cash-out LTV limits

This is one of the more common surprises for investors scaling a portfolio: the reserve requirement on your fifth rental can be meaningfully higher than it was on your first, even if the new property's own numbers look identical, because the lender is weighing your total exposure across every financed property, not just the one in front of them.

Where Reserve Funds Can Come From

  • Checking, savings, and money market account balances -- documented with recent statements, not a screenshot or a printout you've edited
  • A discounted portion of stock, bond, or brokerage account balances
  • A discounted portion of vested retirement account balances in many cases, though early-withdrawal penalties are usually factored in
  • Gift funds, where the lender's program allows them -- documentation requirements are typically stricter for reserves than for a down payment gift
  • Seller-paid closing costs do not help your reserve position -- they reduce what you need to bring to close, not what you need to hold afterward

How to Reduce What You're Required to Hold

A stronger DSCR ratio, a lower loan-to-value, and a clean payment history across any properties you already own are the levers that most directly reduce a reserve requirement, since they all point to the same thing a lender is trying to measure: how much cushion this specific deal actually needs. If a reserve requirement is the sticking point on a deal that otherwise works, ask your lender which of those levers would move the number before assuming the deal is dead.

The Bottom Line

Reserves aren't a formality -- they're often the line item that determines whether you can actually close on a deal you've otherwise qualified for. Ask for the exact reserve requirement in months of PITIA as soon as you start underwriting a property, not after you're already under contract, so it factors into how much cash you keep uncommitted through closing.
