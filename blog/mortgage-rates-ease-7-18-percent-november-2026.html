Mortgage Rates Eased to 7.18% in Early November: A Third Straight Weekly Decline

November 8, 2026 5 min read

After a run that took the 30-year fixed rate to its highest level in nearly three years, rates have now pulled back for three straight weeks. Freddie Mac's November 5 survey put the average at 7.18% -- still elevated by recent-year standards, but a real reversal from the mid-October peak. For investors weighing a sale against a refinance right now, the direction of the move matters as much as the number itself.

The Numbers

Freddie Mac PMMS, November 5, 2026: 30-year fixed: 7.18% (down from 7.24% the prior week)  |  15-year fixed: 6.57%  |  Third consecutive weekly decline  |  Down from a mid-October peak near 7.40%

Per Freddie Mac's November 5 release, this marks the third straight week of declines after rates touched roughly 7.40% in mid-October -- the highest level investors had seen in nearly three years at the time. A pullback of roughly 20 basis points over three weeks is modest in absolute terms, but it's a meaningful change in direction after a stretch where nearly every weekly print had moved higher.

Why a Pullback Doesn't Mean "Back to Normal"

Three weeks of declines is a real trend, not noise -- but 7.18% is still well above where rates sat for most of the first half of 2026, and it follows a softer October jobs report for residential construction and the Federal Reserve's decision to hold its target range in late October rather than cut further. Treat this as rates easing off a multi-year high, not as a return to a low-rate environment. The buyer pool and refinance math at 7.18% still look very different than they did when rates were in the mid-6% range earlier this year.

What a Genuine Pullback Means for Your Deal

  • If you're weighing selling a finished flip versus refinancing into a DSCR rental, re-run both numbers at today's rate rather than the peak rate you underwrote against a few weeks ago -- a deal that looked tight at 7.40% may clear comfortably at 7.18%
  • A modest rate easing can widen your pool of mortgage-qualified retail buyers slightly, which can translate to fewer price cuts needed to move a finished property
  • Don't assume the trend continues in a straight line -- lock in financing or a sale price when the numbers work rather than waiting for a rate you can't be sure arrives
  • If your DSCR qualification was borderline at the October peak, re-test it now; a roughly 20-basis-point improvement in rate directly lowers monthly debt service and can move a marginal deal over the qualifying line

Tracking the Reversal

Mid-October~7.40% -- the recent peak, the highest level in nearly three years
October 297.24% -- the first weekly decline of the current streak
November 57.18% -- the third straight weekly decline

The Bottom Line

Rates are moving in the right direction for the first time in months, but a three-week pullback from a near-three-year high is a change in trend, not a return to cheap financing. Re-underwrite any deal you're sitting on against the current 7.18% print rather than the peak you may have planned around in October, and treat any further easing as a bonus rather than something to count on before you commit to an exit.
