Hard Money vs. DSCR Loans: Which Financing Fits Your Strategy

November 8, 2026 6 min read

New investors often ask which loan is "better" -- hard money or DSCR. That's the wrong question. The two products aren't competing for the same deal; they're built to solve two different problems. Get clear on which problem you actually have, and the choice mostly makes itself.

Two Different Jobs

A hard money loan is short-term bridge financing for a property that isn't finished yet -- a purchase-and-rehab project, a ground-up build, or any deal where the plan is to transform the asset and exit within months, not years. A DSCR loan is long-term financing for a property that's already stabilized and producing (or ready to produce) rental income. One funds the transformation; the other funds the hold.

The one-line test: If the property needs work before it can support itself on rent, you need hard money. If it's already rent-ready and you're financing it to hold, you need a DSCR loan.

How They Qualify You Differently

Hard money underwriting centers on the deal -- purchase price, scope of work, and after-repair value (ARV) -- with less weight on your personal income. DSCR underwriting centers on the property's own cash flow: whether projected or in-place rent covers the loan's monthly debt service, independent of your tax returns or employment history. Neither product asks for two years of pay stubs and W-2s the way a conventional mortgage does, but they're evaluating entirely different things to get there.

What it financesHard Money: purchase + rehab, or ground-up construction  |  DSCR: a stabilized, rent-producing rental
Typical termHard Money: 6-18 months, interest-only  |  DSCR: 30-year fixed or ARM, fully amortizing
Rate rangeHard Money: roughly 9%-13%  |  DSCR: roughly 6.0%-8.75%
Qualifying metricHard Money: deal economics (LTC, ARV)  |  DSCR: the property's debt service coverage ratio
LeverageHard Money: commonly 70%-90% of cost, capped at 65%-80% of ARV  |  DSCR: typically up to 75%-80% LTV depending on the ratio
ExitHard Money: sell or refinance before the term ends  |  DSCR: hold, or refinance later for better terms or cash-out

Where the Line Gets Blurry: The BRRRR Investor

Investors running the buy-rehab-rent-refinance-repeat (BRRRR) strategy actually use both products in sequence, not instead of one another. Hard money funds the acquisition and rehab; once the property is renovated, leased, and has seasoned for the lender's required holding period, a DSCR cash-out refinance pays off the hard money balance and converts the deal into long-term rental financing. Understanding both products -- and how the handoff between them works -- matters more for this strategy than picking a single "better" one.

Common Mistakes Investors Make Choosing Between Them

  • Trying to put DSCR financing on a property that still needs significant rehab -- DSCR lenders generally want a property that's already rentable, not a work-in-progress
  • Staying on a hard money loan too long past your project's completion, racking up extension fees and higher short-term interest instead of refinancing into a DSCR loan once the property qualifies
  • Comparing the two products on rate alone -- a 9%-13% hard money rate on a 7-month bridge loan is a different cost conversation than a 6.0%-8.75% DSCR rate amortized over 30 years
  • Assuming a property that cash-flowed at the rent you underwrote will automatically clear DSCR qualification -- get a realistic rent estimate before you count on the refinance to work

How to Decide

Ask what the property needs right now, not what you eventually plan to do with it. If it needs renovation, new construction, or any work before it can be leased at market rent, you're in hard money territory regardless of your long-term hold intentions. Once it's rent-ready and the numbers pencil on a DSCR basis, that's the point to refinance -- not before, and not long after.

The Bottom Line

Hard money and DSCR loans aren't rivals -- they're sequential tools for two different phases of the same investment. Know which phase your property is actually in before you shop rate, and the right product becomes obvious.
