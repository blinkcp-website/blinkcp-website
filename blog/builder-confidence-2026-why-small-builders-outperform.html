Builder Confidence Is Stuck Below 40 — Why Small Custom Builders Are Still Winning

August 29, 2026 5 min read

The NAHB/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index ticked up one point to 35 in August 2026 — technically an improvement, but the index has now stayed below the neutral 40 mark for 16 consecutive months. Read only the headline number and ground-up construction looks like a hard business to be in right now. Look at who's actually reporting strong conditions inside that same data, and the picture changes.

The Headline Number Hides a Real Split

NAHB's own reporting on the August index is explicit about this: custom home builders continue to report stronger market conditions than spec builders, and smaller, less dense markets are outperforming larger metros. Smaller builders also report relatively stronger conditions than larger builders. The weakness in the topline number is concentrated in large-scale speculative production, not in custom, one-off, or small-batch construction.

Why Large Spec Builders Are Struggling More

Large production builders carry standing inventory — spec homes built ahead of a confirmed buyer, financed the entire time they sit unsold. In an affordability-constrained market, that inventory sits longer, which is exactly why 35% of builders cut prices in August and 63% were using sales incentives, both according to NAHB. A custom or build-to-suit project doesn't carry that same exposure: the buyer (or the investor's own exit plan) is typically identified before the vertical build even starts.

What's Actually Driving the Softness

Elevated mortgage rates, rising construction costs, and broader economic uncertainty are the three forces NAHB cites as weighing on builder sentiment — with rising gas and diesel prices specifically called out as pushing material and delivery costs higher.

None of those three forces disappear for a small builder. But a smaller project has fewer places for cost overruns to compound, a shorter timeline between breaking ground and reaching a sellable or rentable asset, and — critically — a financing structure that can be sized to the deal instead of carrying a large spec portfolio through a slow market.

What This Means for Your Next Deal

  • A soft topline builder-confidence number is not the same signal for a one-lot infill build as it is for a 200-home subdivision — treat market commentary accordingly
  • Smaller, less dense markets are outperforming major metros right now, which is worth weighing when you're choosing where to build next, not just what to build
  • Because larger builders are cutting prices and leaning on incentives to move standing inventory, a completed custom or small-scale spec build competing against that inventory needs a genuinely differentiated product or price point
  • Construction financing that funds in staged draws — rather than one lump sum — limits how much cost exposure you're carrying at any single point in the build, which matters more, not less, when material costs are volatile

The Bottom Line

A national builder-confidence index reads like a single verdict on the industry, but it's really an average across very different businesses. If you're building one project at a time with a defined exit, the softness in the headline number is largely someone else's problem — the large-scale spec builders carrying standing inventory through a slow-rate environment. Your underwriting should reflect the segment you're actually in.
