Cash Buyers Are Pulling Back: What NAR's August Data Means for Sourcing Deals

October 13, 2026 5 min read

Every investor who's lost a deal to a buyer who can close in ten days with no financing contingency knows exactly who they're competing against at the offer stage. That competitive picture is shifting. NAR's August 2026 existing-home sales report shows cash buyers retreating and first-time, financed buyers taking a larger share of the market — a genuine change in who shows up on the other side of a bidding situation.

The August 2026 Numbers

NAR, August 2026: Existing-home sales: 3.98 million annualized (down 2.0% from July)  |  Median existing-home price: $429,100 (up 1.6% year-over-year, the 38th straight month of annual gains)  |  All-cash share: 27% of sales (down from 28% a year ago)  |  First-time buyer share: 30% (up from 28% a year ago)

Per NAR's August 2026 Existing-Home Sales report, the cash share has now ticked down two years in a row even as first-time buyers — who are overwhelmingly financed, not cash — grew their share of the market. That's a different buyer pool than the one investors have been pricing their offers against for the better part of this cycle.

Why Cash Share Is Falling While First-Time Buyers Rise

Cash buyers include a mix of retirees, downsizers, and investors — groups whose purchasing power is sensitive to where their own capital is tied up, not just to mortgage rates. As that pool has thinned, first-time buyers — who need financing almost by definition — have stepped into a larger share of transactions, helped by more available inventory and a market that's finally giving buyers some room to negotiate rather than waive every contingency to compete.

What Less Cash Competition Means for Financed Investors

If you've been structuring offers around the assumption that you're bidding against an all-cash buyer who can beat your terms on speed and certainty alone, that assumption is getting weaker. A rising share of financed, first-time buyers in the mix means more deals where a hard money or DSCR-backed offer with a tight, realistic closing timeline is competitive on more than just price — and where sellers are less able to hold out for an all-cash, no-contingency offer as the default expectation.

  • Lean harder on speed-to-close as a differentiator — it still beats what most first-time, financed buyers can offer, even if it no longer beats every buyer in the pool
  • Revisit markets where you previously assumed cash buyers would out-compete you on acquisition; that calculus may have shifted since you last ran it
  • Don't assume every listing is getting multiple all-cash offers — ask your agent for the actual offer mix on comparable recent sales before you price your bid
  • Use the still-meaningful cash share (27%) as a floor, not a ceiling, when you're estimating how much competition to expect on a given property

The Price Deceleration Underneath the Headline

A 1.6% year-over-year median price gain is a 38th consecutive month of increases, but it's a far cry from the double-digit annual gains of a few years ago. Combined with sales down 2.0% month-over-month, the report describes a market that's still appreciating, just slowly — which matters directly for your ARV assumptions and exit pricing. Don't underwrite a flip or a refinance on the price trajectory of two or three years ago; underwrite it on what this report is actually showing today.

The Bottom Line

A thinning cash-buyer pool and a growing first-time buyer share don't mean competition for deals has disappeared — they mean the nature of that competition has changed. Financed offers with a realistic, fast closing timeline are more competitive against this buyer mix than they were a year or two ago, and that's worth factoring into both your sourcing strategy and your offer terms.
