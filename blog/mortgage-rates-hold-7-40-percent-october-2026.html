Mortgage Rates Hold at 7.40%, the Highest in Nearly Three Years: What a Rate Plateau Means for Investors

October 16, 2026 5 min read

For six straight weeks, the story was the same: rates went up again. That changed in mid-October. Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey put the 30-year fixed rate at 7.40% for the week of October 15, 2026 -- unchanged from the week before, and the first week since August that the average didn't move higher. The level itself is still the highest in nearly three years. But a plateau, even a high one, is a different signal for investors than a continued climb, and it changes how you should think about locking, qualifying, and timing an exit.

The Numbers This Week

Freddie Mac PMMS, week of October 15, 2026: 30-year fixed rate: 7.40% (unchanged from the prior week)  |  15-year fixed rate: 6.71% (down from 6.73%)  |  One year earlier: 6.27%

A year-over-year gap of more than a full point is significant on its own. But the more immediate story is the break in momentum: after rising every week since mid-August, the average held flat. Multiple outlets covering the release, including WFTV's report on Freddie Mac's data, described the current level as the highest in nearly three years -- but also noted it's no longer accelerating.

Rate coverage through the run-up pointed to the same two drivers repeatedly: a sustained rise in Treasury yields and inflation data that's come in hotter than markets expected. Neither of those pressures has fully reversed, which is why the 30-year average is holding near 7.40% rather than pulling back -- but the pace of new upward pressure has at least paused for now.

Why a Plateau Matters More Than the Level

A rate that's still climbing is a moving target: every week you wait to lock, you're betting against an unknown direction. A rate that's plateaued, even at an elevated level, is something you can actually underwrite against with confidence. That distinction matters most for two groups of investors: those sizing a DSCR refinance and those modeling a flip's exit math, both of which depend on knowing what the market will look like in 60-90 days, not just today.

What It Means for DSCR Qualifying

DSCR loans aren't priced directly off the Freddie Mac 30-year average, but hard money and DSCR pricing tend to track the same broader rate environment with a lag. When conventional rates were climbing every week, that pricing was a moving target too -- you could run your DSCR math today and find the deal priced differently by the time you closed. A plateau gives you a more stable number to underwrite against. It doesn't mean rates are about to fall; it means the number you run today is more likely to still be accurate when you close, which matters when a deal is sitting close to a 1.0 DSCR threshold.

What It Means for Fix & Flip Exit Timing

Every weekly rate increase through September and early October shrank the pool of retail buyers who could qualify for a purchase -- which is part of why inventory has been building and days on market have stretched out this fall. A rate that's stopped climbing doesn't shrink that buyer pool further. If your flip is close to finished, that's modestly good news: the backdrop you're selling into this week is unlikely to be meaningfully worse by the time you list, even if it isn't getting easier either.

  • Don't assume a plateau means rates are about to drop -- sticky inflation and elevated Treasury yields haven't resolved, they've just stopped worsening for now
  • If a DSCR deal is marginal on cash flow, a stable-rate environment is a better time to lock than a climbing one, since the number you underwrite is less likely to move against you before closing
  • Re-run your flip's exit comps against current days-on-market and price-reduction data, not data from the summer, since buyer pool size has shifted even if rates haven't moved further this week
  • Watch Freddie Mac's Thursday release for confirmation the plateau is holding before assuming the trend has genuinely reversed

The Bottom Line

Rates are still high -- the highest in nearly three years by multiple measures -- but for the first time since the summer, they stopped climbing week over week. That's a meaningfully different environment to underwrite in than a market where the number keeps moving against you every time you check it. Use the stability to lock in financing with confidence, not to wait for a rate relief that the data doesn't yet support.
