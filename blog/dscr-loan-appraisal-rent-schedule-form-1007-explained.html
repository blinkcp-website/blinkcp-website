How DSCR Lenders Verify Rent: The Appraisal and Form 1007 Rent Schedule Explained

November 8, 2026 6 min read

A DSCR loan qualifies on rent, not on your income -- which means the rent figure the lender actually uses matters more than almost anything else in your application. Investors are often surprised to learn that figure doesn't come from the lease they hand the loan officer, or the number they saw on a listing site. It comes from an appraiser, using a specific form built for exactly this purpose.

Lease Rent vs. Appraised Market Rent

If a property is already tenant-occupied with a signed lease, most DSCR lenders will use the lower of the actual lease rent or the appraiser's market rent estimate. If the property is vacant or you're buying it with a tenant you plan to replace, the appraiser's market rent figure is typically the only number the lender has to work with. Either way, the appraisal is doing real underwriting work here -- it's not just confirming the property's value, it's independently verifying the income side of your DSCR calculation.

Why it matters: A DSCR of 1.15 calculated off your own optimistic rent estimate means nothing if the appraiser's comparable rent schedule comes back $200/month lower -- that's often the difference between a loan that clears and one that doesn't.

Form 1007: The Single-Family Rent Schedule

For a single-family rental, the appraiser completes Fannie Mae Form 1007, the Single-Family Comparable Rent Schedule, alongside the standard appraisal. The appraiser pulls three or more comparable rental listings or leases in the immediate area, adjusts for differences in size, condition, and amenities, and arrives at an opinion of fair market rent for your specific property -- the same comps-and-adjustments process used for a sales-value appraisal, just applied to rent instead of price.

Form 1025: The 2-4 Unit Version

For a 2-4 unit property, the appraiser instead completes Form 1025, the Small Residential Income Property Appraisal Report, which documents rent for each unit individually along with an operating income statement. Multi-unit rent verification is naturally more detailed, since the lender needs a per-unit rent roll to properly calculate total property income, not just a single blended number.

What Appraisers Actually Look At

  • Recently leased comparable properties within the same submarket, adjusted for bedroom/bathroom count, square footage, and condition
  • Whether the subject property's finishes and systems (kitchen, bath, HVAC, roof) are at, above, or below the comps they're pulling
  • Any functional differences -- garage, outdoor space, in-unit laundry -- that move rent up or down relative to the comps
  • For an occupied property, the existing lease terms, to compare against the market-rent conclusion

How to Prepare Before the Appraiser Shows Up

Make sure the property is accessible and in presentable condition for the appraisal appointment -- a rehab with exposed systems or incomplete finishes can pull the appraiser's rent conclusion down even if the work is nearly done. If you have a signed lease, have it ready; if the unit is vacant, pulling your own comparable rental listings ahead of time (same bed/bath count, similar condition, nearby) can help you sanity-check the number you're underwriting to before the report comes back.

What to Do If the Appraised Rent Comes in Low

A low rent schedule hits your DSCR ratio directly, since rent is the numerator in the calculation. Before assuming the deal is dead, ask your lender whether a reconsideration of value is possible with updated comparable rents, since appraisers do make errors on comp selection. If the number holds, your options are the same as any DSCR shortfall: a larger down payment to lower the loan amount, a rate buydown with points, or walking away from a deal that doesn't actually cash flow at the rent the market will really support.

The Bottom Line

Your DSCR math is only as reliable as the rent figure behind it, and that figure comes from an appraiser's comparable rent schedule, not your own estimate. Know this going in, pull your own market comps before you underwrite a deal, and you'll rarely be surprised by what Form 1007 or Form 1025 comes back with.
