September Home Sales Ticked Up -- But Inventory and Days on Market Keep Climbing

October 25, 2026 5 min read

On the surface, the National Association of Realtors' September 2026 report looks like good news: existing-home sales rose 1.5% from August. Look one line down, though, and the picture is less reassuring -- sales are still down 2.2% from a year ago, inventory keeps building, and homes are sitting on the market longer than they were even a month earlier. A monthly bounce inside a slower year is a different signal than genuine momentum, and it changes how you should read both sides of your next deal: what you pay to buy it, and how long you should expect to hold a finished one.

The September 2026 Numbers

NAR, September 2026 (released October 13, 2026): Sales: 3.99 million units, seasonally adjusted annual rate (+1.5% month over month, -2.2% year over year)  |  Inventory: 1.60 million units (+1.9% month over month, +4.6% year over year)  |  Months' supply: 4.8  |  Median price: $417,500 (+1.3% year over year)  |  Median days on market: 33 (up from 31 in August)

NAR Chief Economist Lawrence Yun noted that August and September marked the two slowest months for home sales this year, and pointed to the recent run-up in mortgage rates as the reason fewer buyers currently qualify to purchase. That context matters: the monthly sales gain happened in spite of a weakening buyer pool, not because demand picked up.

A Monthly Gain That Masks a Slower Market

Month-over-month figures are seasonally adjusted, which smooths out the normal fall slowdown -- but a 1.5% monthly increase sitting on top of a 2.2% year-over-year decline tells you the adjustment, not the underlying trend, is doing most of the work in that headline number. Sales are still meaningfully lower than they were a year ago, at a time when rates were also lower. That combination is a clear signal that elevated financing costs, not seasonal noise, are the main thing holding transaction volume down.

Inventory and Days on Market: The Real Story

Inventory climbing 4.6% year over year to 1.60 million units, alongside a jump in median days on market from 31 to 33, is the more telling pair of numbers in this report. Supply is building faster than homes are selling, which is exactly the setup that shifts negotiating leverage toward buyers -- including investors sourcing deals. A 4.8-month supply isn't a buyer's market by the classic six-month definition, but it's trending in that direction, and it's the highest reading of the current cycle in most of the markets tracking it.

What It Means for Sourcing Your Next Flip

More inventory sitting longer typically means more sellers willing to negotiate on price, concessions, or timeline -- particularly on properties that have already logged a few weeks on market without an offer. That's worth building into your sourcing strategy directly: properties at 30+ days on market are increasingly common rather than the exception, and a patient, disciplined offer often beats a rushed one in a market shifting this way.

What It Means for Your Exit Timeline

The flip side applies when you're the one selling a finished flip or a build. A median of 33 days on market is a statewide average -- plenty of local markets are running longer -- and it's worth underwriting your holding costs and interest reserves against a 45-60 day marketing period rather than the fast two-to-three week sales some investors got used to earlier in the cycle. Pricing a finished property realistically from day one, rather than testing the market high and chasing it down, matters more in a market where supply is building.

The Regional Split

NAR's report showed month-over-month sales rising in the South and West, holding roughly flat in the Midwest, and declining in the Northeast -- while every region was down on a year-over-year basis. That regional divergence means the national averages above are a starting point, not a substitute for pulling current local data before you price a listing or size a holding-cost reserve.

  • Treat the monthly sales gain as a seasonal adjustment artifact, not a sign of genuinely stronger demand, given the year-over-year decline underneath it
  • Use rising days on market as leverage when making offers on inventory that's already sat for several weeks
  • Underwrite exit timelines against 45-60 days on market rather than last year's faster sales pace
  • Pull your specific metro's months-of-supply figure rather than relying on the 4.8-month national average -- regional variation is wide right now
  • Size interest reserves on fix and flip and construction loans assuming a longer marketing period than you may have needed a year ago

The Bottom Line

September's headline sales gain is real but narrow -- a modest monthly bounce inside a market that's still slower than a year ago, with more inventory and longer marketing times building underneath it. For investors, that combination cuts both ways: more negotiating room on the buy side, and more patience required on the sell side. Price and plan for the market this data actually describes, not the faster one from a year ago.
