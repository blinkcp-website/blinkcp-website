DSCR Loans on 2-4 Unit Properties: How Small Multifamily Underwriting Differs

October 18, 2026 5 min read

A duplex rents a little differently than a single-family house, and it finances a little differently too. DSCR loans cover 2-4 unit properties the same way they cover single-family rentals -- qualifying the property on its income rather than your personal tax returns -- but the mechanics of calculating that income, underwriting vacancy, and documenting leases get more involved once you're dealing with multiple doors under one roof. Here's what actually changes.

Income Is Calculated Per Unit, Then Combined

On a single-family DSCR loan, there's one rent figure to work with. On a duplex, triplex, or fourplex, the lender totals the rental income across all units -- whether that's actual signed leases, a market rent schedule, or a combination of both if some units are occupied and others are vacant at closing. A fourplex with units renting for $1,400, $1,350, $1,450, and $1,300 contributes $5,500 in combined monthly gross rent to the DSCR calculation, the same way a single-family property's one lease would.

DSCR = Combined Monthly Rental Income (all units) ÷ Monthly Debt Service (PITIA)
The numerator changes from one rent figure to a sum across units; the formula itself doesn't.

Vacancy Risk Is Underwritten Differently

This is where multi-unit properties actually behave differently, not just mathematically but practically. A single-family rental that loses its tenant goes from fully occupied to fully vacant overnight -- zero income until it's re-leased. A fourplex that loses one tenant out of four keeps collecting rent on the other three. That diversified income stream is a real underwriting advantage multi-unit properties have over single-family rentals, and it's part of why many investors view small multifamily as a more resilient cash-flow asset, even though each individual unit may rent for less than a comparable single-family home.

Documentation Gets More Involved

Single-family DSCROne lease (or one market rent estimate) to document and verify
2-4 unit DSCRA lease or rent estimate for each unit individually, plus a rent roll showing occupancy status unit by unit
Mixed occupancy at closingLenders typically blend actual lease income for occupied units with market rent for vacant ones, rather than requiring full occupancy to close
Utility and expense structureLenders will ask who pays utilities, trash, and common-area maintenance per unit -- it affects the net income picture, especially if the owner covers utilities rather than tenants

Leverage and Pricing Considerations

DSCR leverage on 2-4 unit properties is generally similar to single-family DSCR leverage, though individual lender guidelines vary and some price multi-unit properties slightly differently to reflect property management complexity. The bigger practical difference tends to show up in the appraisal: multi-unit properties are typically valued using an income approach alongside sales comparisons, so the appraiser's own rent schedule becomes part of your DSCR math, not just your own lease documentation.

Why Investors Scale Into Small Multifamily

Buying one fourplex consolidates four rent checks, four leases, and four maintenance relationships under a single loan, a single property tax bill, and often a single roof and furnace -- which can be more capital-efficient to manage than four scattered single-family rentals, even though the per-door cash flow may look similar on paper. For investors scaling a DSCR portfolio, 2-4 unit properties are frequently a deliberate strategy to add doors without adding proportional complexity.

  • Pull or estimate rent for every unit individually before you underwrite the deal, not just a blended average
  • Confirm who pays utilities and common-area costs per unit -- it changes your effective net income and your DSCR
  • Ask your lender how they'll treat a vacant unit at closing, since policies on blending actual and market rent vary
  • Request the rent roll from the seller early in due diligence, including lease start and end dates for every unit
  • Factor in that multi-unit properties often carry higher insurance and maintenance costs per property, even if lower per unit, when sizing your debt service

The Bottom Line

DSCR financing scales naturally from a single-family rental to a 2-4 unit property because the underlying question stays the same: does the income cover the debt? What changes is the detail underneath that question -- per-unit documentation, vacancy diversification, and how utilities and expenses are split. Get those details right during underwriting, and a duplex or fourplex can qualify just as cleanly as a single-family rental, often with a more resilient income stream behind it.
