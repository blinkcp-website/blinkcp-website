Pending Home Sales Fell 7.3% in September: What a Weakening Leading Indicator Means for Your Flip's Exit Window

November 1, 2026 5 min read

The National Association of Realtors' Pending Home Sales Index fell 7.3% month over month and 11.3% year over year in September, dropping to a reading of 66.0. All four U.S. regions declined. It's a quieter release than a jobs report or a Fed decision, but if you're sitting on a flip you plan to list in the next month or two, it's arguably more relevant to your exit than either one.

What the Pending Home Sales Index Actually Measures

Unlike existing-home sales data, which counts transactions that have already closed, the Pending Home Sales Index tracks signed contracts -- homes that are under agreement but haven't closed yet. Because there's typically a 30- to 60-day lag between contract signing and closing, pending sales function as a leading indicator: September's contract activity is a preview of what October and November's closed-sales numbers are likely to show. A sharp drop here means buyer demand was already cooling before the headline closed-sales reports caught up to it.

The September Numbers

National index66.0, down 7.3% from August and 11.3% from September 2025
WestDown roughly 9.2% month over month and 15% year over year -- the weakest region
SouthDown roughly 8.2% month over month and 12% year over year
Midwest & NortheastAlso declined, though by smaller margins than the West and South

NAR Chief Economist Lawrence Yun attributed the pullback directly to financing costs, saying market conditions weakened in September due to fast-rising mortgage rates. That lines up with Freddie Mac's data for the same period, which showed the 30-year fixed average climbing through September before peaking near 7.40% in mid-October.

Why This Matters More If You're Flipping Than If You're Holding

A DSCR rental purchase isn't directly exposed to this number -- you're not selling into retail buyer demand, you're financing against rental income. But if your business model depends on selling a finished rehab to an owner-occupant or a retail investor, a falling pending-sales index means the buyer pool you're listing into in the coming weeks is thinner and more rate-sensitive than it was over the summer. Days on market stretch, price reductions become more common, and buyers negotiate harder on condition and closing costs.

How to Adjust Your Underwriting and Listing Plan

  • Price your projected exit off current pending-contract comps, not the last closed sale from two months ago -- it's already stale in a market moving this fast
  • Build extra carrying cost into your reserve for a longer days-on-market window than you budgeted in the spring
  • Have a pre-planned price-reduction cadence (e.g., a review point at 30 and 45 days on market) instead of reacting emotionally once it happens
  • Revisit your sell-or-refinance decision framework earlier in the project, not after the rehab is finished and you're already on the clock
  • If your loan has an extension option, know its cost before you need it -- not after your maturity date is a week away

Where the Slowdown Cuts the Other Way

A softer buyer pool on the sell side often comes with a softer seller pool on the acquisition side. Fewer competing buyers at the table, more sellers willing to negotiate on price or terms, and less bidding-war pressure on your next purchase. If you're sourcing your next deal right now, a cooling pending-sales print is a signal to negotiate harder on the way in, even as you plan more conservatively on the way out.

The Bottom Line

A 7.3% monthly drop in signed contracts is a real signal, not noise -- it tells you what closed-sales data will likely show in a month or two. Build your exit assumptions around where demand is heading, not where it was over the summer, and use the acquisition-side opportunity this kind of market creates.
