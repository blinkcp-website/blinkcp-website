Short-Term Rental DSCR Loans: Qualifying an Airbnb on Projected Income

September 26, 2026 6 min read

A long-term rental DSCR loan qualifies a property against a lease. A short-term rental doesn't have one -- it has a booking calendar. STR DSCR loans exist to bridge that gap, letting investors finance an Airbnb or vacation rental against its projected or actual nightly income instead of a 12-month lease. The underwriting is genuinely different, and getting it wrong is the fastest way to have a deal fall apart at the term sheet stage.

How STR Income Actually Gets Underwritten

Lenders qualify STR income one of two ways: actual performance, using 12 months of host platform statements (Airbnb, VRBO) showing real booking revenue, or projected performance, using a third-party market rent report -- typically from AirDNA -- that estimates what a comparable property in that market would generate. When a lender relies on a projection rather than a track record, it's standard practice to apply a haircut, discounting the projected gross revenue to somewhere around 75%–80% before dividing by 12 to get the monthly income figure used in the DSCR calculation.

STR DSCR income, projection-based: AirDNA projected annual gross revenue × ~75–80% haircut ÷ 12 = qualifying monthly income used in the DSCR formula. A property with 12+ months of actual host statements can often qualify off real numbers instead.

What Counts as a Good STR DSCR Right Now

Because short-term rental income carries more seasonality and platform risk than a signed lease, lenders generally want to see a stronger cushion than they would on a long-term rental file. Strong-credit borrowers with well-documented performance in established markets have been able to reach DSCRs in the 1.25–1.65 range in top-performing STR markets, compared to the 1.0–1.25 range that's typical on a standard long-term-lease DSCR loan. Pricing for a clean STR DSCR file -- 720+ credit, roughly 1.15 DSCR, 75% LTV, seasoned 12+ month host history -- has run in a 6.625%–7.500% range through mid-2026 for well-seasoned properties in lower-regulation markets, with weaker files or heavier regulatory exposure pricing higher.

Why STR Growth Slowing Down Is Actually Good News for Buyers

AirDNA's 2026 Short-Term Rental Outlook projects U.S. available STR listing growth of just 4.6% for the year, a fraction of the roughly 20% annual expansion the industry saw at its 2021–2022 peak. Slower new-supply growth means less new competition flooding markets that are already established, which is part of why AirDNA frames 2026 as a window with real opportunity for investors entering STR markets that were oversaturated a few years ago.

Where Regulation Risk Gets Priced In

Unlike a long-term rental, an STR's legality can change with a single city council vote -- a permit cap, an owner-occupancy requirement, or an outright ban. Lenders account for this by looking at the specific jurisdiction's STR ordinance status, not just the property's numbers: a market with stable, established STR permitting supports higher leverage and better pricing than one with active regulatory proposals pending. Nashville, for example, has remained a top-tier STR market in 2026 in part because its regulatory framework is comparatively settled, while markets facing new restriction proposals see lenders tighten leverage or price in the added risk.

Before You Apply

  • Pull an AirDNA market report for the specific property, not the metro average -- STR revenue varies block to block near key attractions
  • If you already operate the property or a comparable one, gather 12 months of platform statements; actual performance underwrites more favorably than a projection in most cases
  • Confirm the property's STR legality and any permit or registration requirements in that specific city before you assume the numbers will qualify
  • Model your DSCR at the haircut-adjusted income, not the raw projected revenue, so you're not surprised by the number your lender actually uses

The Bottom Line

STR DSCR loans open up financing for a property type long-term-lease underwriting was never built for, but the tradeoff is a lender that discounts your income more conservatively and prices in regulatory risk your neighbor's long-term rental doesn't carry. Come in with real booking data or a defensible market report, and know your jurisdiction's STR rules cold before you make an offer.
