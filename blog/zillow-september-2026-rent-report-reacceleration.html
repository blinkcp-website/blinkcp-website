Rent Growth Just Hit a 17-Month High — What It Means for DSCR Underwriting

October 14, 2026 5 min read

Zillow's September 2026 market report, released October 6, put the typical U.S. asking rent at $1,932 — up 2.7% from a year earlier, the fastest annual pace since April 2025. That's not a one-month blip: Zillow's data shows annual rent growth accelerating every month since April, for both single-family and multifamily rentals. If you underwrote a DSCR deal earlier this year expecting rents to keep softening, this report is worth a second look.

The Forecast That Didn't Hold

Earlier this year, Zillow's own outlook projected single-family rent growth decelerating to roughly 1.1% annually by December, and a lot of investor underwriting leaned on that assumption — treat rents as flat-to-slowing, don't bank on them bailing out a tight DSCR. The actual trajectory has moved the other way. Rent growth has strengthened in every reading since the spring, and September's 2.7% national figure is well above where the mid-year forecast expected the market to be by now.

That's not a knock on the earlier forecast — rent trends shift with supply absorption, seasonal leasing patterns, and local conditions, and six months is a long time in a market still working through a multifamily construction wave. The point for investors is simpler: a forecast is not a fact, and the only rent number that should drive your underwriting is the one closest to the day you close.

What's Likely Driving the Reacceleration

Zillow's July 2026 data showed just under 40% of listed rentals offering a concession — move-in specials, free months, reduced deposits — as landlords worked through a glut of newly delivered multifamily units. As that oversupply gets absorbed and new deliveries slow from their peak, landlords have less reason to discount, which shows up directly in asking-rent growth. Single-family rentals, which never saw the same construction surge as multifamily, have held firmer throughout.

Single-Family vs. Multifamily: Not the Same Market

This is the split that matters most for 1-4 unit DSCR investors: the asset class you're financing has been outperforming the broader apartment market on rent growth, and the gap is expected to persist through year-end. Comping your subject property against overall "rental market" headlines, rather than the single-family segment specifically, can understate what a property can actually support.

What This Means for DSCR Underwriting

DSCR qualification lives or dies on one input: the rent figure used in the ratio. A property priced off last spring's softer comps may already be underpriced relative to where asking rents have moved. Before you lock in a lowball number out of caution, or walk away from a deal that looked marginal six months ago, it's worth re-pulling current comps.

Worth rechecking if: you ran DSCR numbers on a property between April and August using rent comps pulled before this acceleration — the market may have moved in your favor since.

How to Pressure-Test Your Rent Assumption Today

  • Pull comps dated within the last 30-60 days, not comps from earlier in the year
  • Separate single-family comps from multifamily/apartment comps — they're moving at different speeds
  • Check whether nearby listings are still offering concessions, which signals the local market hasn't caught up to the national trend yet
  • Get a signed lease in hand before closing where possible — documented income underwrites more cleanly than a market estimate, especially in a market that's moving
  • Don't assume the September pace continues indefinitely — build in a cushion rather than underwriting to the hottest recent print

The Bottom Line

Rent growth has reaccelerated for five straight months and is running hotter than this year's own forecasts expected, with single-family rentals leading the way. That's a tailwind for DSCR qualification on properties priced using current data — but only if your comps are actually current. Run fresh numbers before you decide a deal doesn't pencil.
