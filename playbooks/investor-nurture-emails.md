# Investor Nurture Series — 5 emails

For real estate investors who have opted in: downloaded the DSCR calculator,
subscribed, met you at a REIA meeting, or enquired and gone quiet. Warm, not
cold — these people know who you are.

Different job from `lead-cadence.md`. That one chases a specific deal. This one
earns attention over a few weeks so you're the call they make when a deal
appears.

**Send spacing:** days 0, 4, 9, 15, 22. One idea per email, one link.

## Rules

- No rate, point or term in writing. Pricing lives in the term sheet.
- No approval language. "Let's see if it fits" — never "you qualify."
- Every figure below is from `case-studies.html`. Nothing invented.
- CAN-SPAM: physical address and a working unsubscribe on every send.
- "Catchy" for investors means a specific number, not an exclamation mark.
  No ALL CAPS, no "guaranteed", no "$$$" — those land you in spam, which
  matters doubly until SPF and DKIM are fixed (see `email-deliverability.md`).

Merge fields: `{first_name}`, `{advisor}`.

## Email 2 — Day 4 · The mistake

**Subject:** The $62K rehab that nearly died at draw two
**Alt subject:** Your contractor gets paid before your draw does
**Preview:** Budget the gap, not just the rehab.

> {first_name} —
>
> The most common way a fix & flip goes sideways has nothing to do with the
> purchase price.
>
> It's this: your contractor finishes framing and wants paying. Your draw
> reimburses *after* inspection. So for a week or two, that money comes out of
> your pocket — and if you budgeted to the dollar, you stall. A stalled job
> isn't just slow, it's a crew that moves to someone else's project.
>
> The fix is unglamorous: **match your draw stages to your contractor's payment
> schedule before you sign the scope of work.** Not after.
>
> One we funded: $185,000 purchase, $62,000 rehab, ARV $340,000. Loan was
> $208,650 on a 12-month term. It closed in 11 days, and the reason it ran
> clean was that the draw stages and the contractor's invoicing lined up from
> day one.
>
> Full breakdown of how draws work:
> blinkcp.com/blog/fix-and-flip-draw-schedules-explained.html
>
> — {advisor}

*Useful whether or not they ever borrow — which is what makes it warm rather
than an ad.*

## Email 3 — Day 9 · The build

**Subject:** $95K lot. $650K house. One lender.
**Alt subject:** Why builders hate splitting their financing
**Preview:** Land lender plus construction lender equals two closings.

> {first_name} —
>
> A builder had an infill lot under contract and ran into the thing that makes
> ground-up annoying: the land lender and the construction lender are usually
> two different people. Two applications, two closings, two sets of fees, and a
> gap in between where the project sits still.
>
> How it actually went:
>
> - Lot: **$95,000**
> - Construction budget: **$410,000**
> - Loan: **$454,500** — **90% of total project cost**
> - Term: **18 months**
> - ARV once built: **$650,000**
>
> One loan covering land and vertical, drawn in stages against inspections.
>
> If you're sitting on a lot and doing the two-lender maths right now, that's
> the conversation worth having before you're under contract, not after.
>
> — {advisor}

*Scannable numbers. Investors read the list and skip the prose — that's fine,
the list carries it.*

## Email 4 — Day 15 · The myth

**Subject:** No tax returns. Genuinely.
**Alt subject:** The rental loan that doesn't look at your W-2
**Preview:** The property qualifies, not your paperwork.

> {first_name} —
>
> The most persistent myth in rental financing: that you need two years of tax
> returns and a tidy W-2 to buy another door.
>
> For DSCR, you don't. The property's income is what qualifies the loan. If the
> rent covers the payment, that's the conversation — your personal tax picture
> largely isn't.
>
> What it looked like on a recent one:
>
> - Property: **$310,000**
> - Rent: **$2,650/month**
> - Loan: **$232,500** at **75% LTV**
> - **No tax returns**
> - Closed in **21 days**
>
> This is the one that changes what's possible for people who are
> self-employed, newly self-employed, or already have enough mortgages that
> conventional has stopped returning their calls.
>
> Run your own numbers: blinkcp.com/dscr-calculator.html
>
> — {advisor}

*Myth-busting works because the reader has believed the myth. Correcting it
earns credibility a testimonial can't.*
